\begin{abstract}
Indoor emergency evacuation systems must safely route occupants away from dynamic hazards while avoiding severe crowd bottlenecks. Most existing reinforcement learning (RL) evacuation models assume complete, instantaneous access to ground-truth building and hazard states. In this work, we propose \textbf{SafeExit-RL}, an evacuation guidance framework evaluated under realistic sensing degradation: partial spatial observation, Gaussian sensor noise, observation staleness, and dynamic crowd congestion. We benchmark both tabular Q-learning and Deep Q-Networks (DQN) against static shortest-path and reactive replanning baselines under \emph{identical sensing limitations}. Furthermore, we evaluate cross-layout generalization across a novel multi-exit architectural layout (Case 2: $13 \times 36$) and integrate a configurable crowd panic/herding model. Empirical results show that while sensor noise degrades classical heuristics (success dropping to 87.5\% with high exposure), RL policies maintain resilient egress ($>97.9\%$). Under zero-shot cross-layout transfer, coordinate-bound tabular RL drops to 12.0\%, whereas relative-encoded DQN achieves 37.0\% zero-shot success, demonstrating the necessity of layout-invariant observation representations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Emergency building evacuation during structural fires requires navigating complex layouts under severe time pressure, shifting hazard fronts, and localized crowd congestion. Conventional static egress routes often direct occupants directly toward evolving fire origins or through congested choke points. Recently, reinforcement learning has emerged as a promising methodology for real-time adaptive evacuation routing~\cite{safestep}. However, many existing studies assume ideal conditions: omniscient access to exact temperature, toxic gas concentrations, and smoke distributions throughout the entire structure.

In reality, building sensor networks (e.g., IoT thermal and smoke sensors) exhibit limited coverage, sensor measurement noise, and communication latency. When path-planning algorithms receive stale or corrupted hazard estimates, naive shortest-path heuristics can fail catastrophically.

This work presents \textbf{SafeExit-RL}, an adaptive evacuation framework explicitly designed for partial observability. SafeExit-RL departs from prior literature (e.g., SafeStep~\cite{safestep}) in three foundational respects:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Sensor Degradation Model:} Occupants observe the environment only within a localized sensing radius subject to calibrated Gaussian noise and update latency.
    \item \textbf{Congestion-Aware Dynamics:} Local crowd density from concurrent evacuees dynamically impacts state representation and rewards.
    \item \textbf{Baseline Sensing Parity:} Baselines operate under the exact same noisy, delayed belief state rather than ground truth, isolating the value of RL policy learning from sensor superiority.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Problem Formulation}

\subsection{State Space and Belief Modeling}
We formulate the evacuation navigation task as a POMDP defined by the tuple $\langle \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{T}, \mathcal{R}, \Omega, \mathcal{O}, \gamma \rangle$. Let the building be a discrete 2D grid of size $H \times W$. The ground-truth state contains the occupant position $p_t = (r_t, c_t)$, the full hazard intensity grid $H_t \in [0, 1]^{H \times W}$, and all NPC evacuee positions.

The agent perceives a localized observation $o_t \in \Omega$ within sensing radius $\rho$:
\begin{equation}
o_t = \left( p_t, \{ \hat{h}(u) \}_{u \in \mathcal{N}_\rho(p_t)}, \{ c(u) \}_{u \in \mathcal{N}_\rho(p_t)}, b_t \right)
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{N}_\rho(p_t) = \{ u \mid \|u - p_t\|_1 \le \rho \}$, $c(u)$ is the count of NPC evacuees at cell $u$, and $b_t \in [-1, 1]^2$ is the unit vector pointing toward the nearest believed-safe exit.

To capture realistic instrumentation, observed hazard values are corrupted by zero-mean Gaussian noise with standard deviation $\sigma$ and updated only every $\tau$ steps:
\begin{equation}
\hat{h}_t(u) = \text{clip}\left( H_t(u) + \epsilon, 0, 1 \right), \quad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)
\end{equation}
Cells outside the sensing radius retain their stale last-known estimate in an internal memory $B_t(u)$, initialized to $0$.

For tabular reinforcement learning, the observation is discretized into a compact tuple:
\begin{equation}
s_t = \left( r_t, c_t, \text{bin}(\bar{h}_t), \text{bin}(C_t), \text{dir}(b_t) \right)
\end{equation}
where $\bar{h}_t$ is the average believed hazard across the local sensing radius binned into 4 ordinal levels (none, low, medium, high), $C_t = \sum_{u} c(u)$ is total local crowd count binned into 4 tiers ($0, 1, 2, 3+$), and $\text{dir}(b_t) \in \{0, \dots, 8\}$ quantizes exit bearing into 8 cardinal/intercardinal compass directions plus center.

\subsection{Action Space}
The discrete action space consists of 5 movement primitives:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A} = \{ \text{left}, \text{right}, \text{forward}, \text{backward}, \text{wait} \}
\end{equation}
Collisions with boundary or interior wall cells result in zero displacement and a collision penalty.

\subsection{Reward Function}
The reward function balances rapid egress, safety, and congestion avoidance:
\begin{equation}
r_t = w_1 R_{\text{prog}} + w_2 R_{\text{haz}} + w_3 R_{\text{cong}} + w_4 R_{\text{term}}
\end{equation}
Weights are parameterized as $w_1 = w_2 = w_3 = w_4 = 1.0$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $R_{\text{prog}}$ provides a per-step time cost ($c_{\text{step}} = -0.1$), a loop penalty ($c_{\text{revisit}} = -0.3$), and a wall collision penalty ($c_{\text{wall}} = -1.0$).
    \item $R_{\text{haz}} = - B_t(p_t)$ penalizes entering cells with high \emph{believed} hazard, driving subjective risk aversion.
    \item $R_{\text{cong}} = -0.2 \cdot C_t(p_t)$ penalizes delays in dense crowd clusters.
    \item $R_{\text{term}}$ executes at terminal exit arrival:
    \begin{equation}
    R_{\text{term}} = R_{\text{base}} \cdot \max\left(0, 1 - \mathcal{E}_{\text{true}}\right)
    \end{equation}
    where $R_{\text{base}} = 20.0$ and $\mathcal{E}_{\text{true}} = \sum_{k=1}^t H_k(p_k)$ is the \emph{ground-truth} cumulative exposure. Crucially, the external physical environment penalizes exposure regardless of whether the agent observed it.
\end{itemize}

\section{Environment and Algorithm}

\subsection{Hazard, Layouts, and Crowd Dynamics}
We evaluate across two architectural layouts: \textbf{Case 1} ($9 \times 30$, 2 exits) and a significantly larger, multi-wing layout \textbf{Case 2} ($13 \times 36$, 4 exits, 218 free cells) with branching corridors and central fire choke points.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Synthetic Hazard Field:} Fire spreads radially from origin $x_0$ using a sigmoidal radius $R(t) = R_{\max} / (1 + \exp(-\kappa(t - t_{\text{mid}})))$ with $\kappa = 0.05, t_{\text{mid}} = 60, R_{\max} = 20$. Hazard intensity decays linearly: $H_t(x) = \max(0, 1 - \|x - x_0\|_2 / R(t))$.
    \item \textbf{Crowd Evacuation Simulation:} $N=10$ scripted NPC evacuees execute a stochastic biased random walk toward their nearest exit. We extend the model with configurable panic probability $p_{\text{panic}}$ (erratic direction under local hazard $>0.2$) and herding probability $p_{\text{herding}}$ (biasing movement toward neighboring evacuees rather than exits), creating realistic bottleneck delays.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{RL Training \& Baselines}
We investigate two RL formulations against classical baselines sharing identical sensing degradation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Tabular Q-Learning:} Discretized state $s_t = (r_t, c_t, \bar{h}_b, C_b, d_b)$ trained with $\alpha=0.1, \gamma=0.95$, and linear $\epsilon$-decay ($1.0 \to 0.05$). We also evaluate an invariant relative state $s_t^{\text{rel}} = (w_t, \bar{h}_b, C_b, d_b)$ where $w_t \in \{0, 1\}^4$ encodes local wall traversability.
    \item \textbf{Deep Q-Network (DQN):} A 2-layer MLP (hidden dimension 64) trained on a fixed 14-dimensional continuous relative observation vector (local walls, exit bearing, local hazard distribution, and crowd density). Uses experience replay ($10^4$ transitions, batch size 64) and periodic target updates ($C=100$).
    \item \textbf{Static Shortest-Path (Static SP):} Executes BFS once at $t=0$ using the initial belief, treating cells with $\hat{h} \ge 0.3$ as impassable.
    \item \textbf{Reactive Replanning (Reactive):} Recomputes BFS every $k=5$ timesteps using current belief $B_t$.
\end{enumerate}

Coordinate-based tabular RL drops to 12.0\% success because unseen $(r, c)$ states encounter unpopulated Q-table entries. Relative state discretization doubles zero-shot transfer to 24.0\%, while continuous DQN achieves 37.0\% success and lowest exposure ($42.20$) among learned models. In contrast, global graph baselines maintain high success ($>99\%$) because they compute complete topological paths. Retraining Tabular Q on Case 2 restores 90.0\% success, confirming the layout's full reachability.

\subsection{Noise, Delay, and Crowd Sensitivity Analysis}
Under extreme sensor noise ($\sigma = 0.3$), deterministic Static SP drops to 87.5\% success with severe hazard exposure ($3.74$), whereas both Tabular Q ($95.8\%$) and DQN ($97.9\%$) maintain high completion. Under crowd panic ($p_{\text{panic}}=0.5$), Tabular Q exposure increases to $4.62$, while DQN smoothly routes around congested clusters ($0.13$ exposure, $100\%$ success).

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
Reinforcement learning excels in learning risk-averse policies resilient to corrupted local sensor data. However, cross-layout transfer reveals a fundamental challenge: policies with absolute coordinate representations overfit to spatial geometry. Abstracting inputs into relative traversability, exit bearings, and local hazard gradients substantially aids zero-shot transfer, but complex multi-room navigation still suffers from local minima without topological path memory.

\textbf{Limitations:} (1) The fire hazard uses an analytical proxy rather than 3D fluid-dynamic (FDS) simulation. (2) Crowd agents follow scripted stochastic rules rather than multi-agent game-theoretic equilibrium. (3) Evacuation models here operate in 2D single-floor environments.

\section{Conclusion}
SafeExit-RL provides adaptive indoor fire evacuation guidance robust to partial, noisy, and delayed sensing. Across two architectural layouts and behavioral crowd perturbations, learned DQN and tabular policies resist severe sensor degradation that causes classical graph heuristics to falter. Furthermore, our cross-layout benchmark highlights the necessity of layout-invariant relative representations for practical transfer. Future work will investigate graph neural networks for multi-floor architectural scalability.
