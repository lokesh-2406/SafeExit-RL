\begin{abstract}
Indoor emergency evacuation systems must safely route occupants away from dynamic hazards while avoiding severe crowd bottlenecks. Most existing reinforcement learning (RL) evacuation models assume complete, instantaneous access to ground-truth building and hazard states. In this work, we propose \textbf{SafeExit-RL}, an evacuation guidance framework evaluated under realistic sensing degradation: partial spatial observation, Gaussian sensor noise, observation staleness, and local crowd congestion. We formulate evacuation as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) solved via tabular Q-learning over a discretized belief state. Crucially, we benchmark our learned policy against static shortest-path and reactive periodic replanning baselines under \emph{identical sensing limitations}. Empirical evaluations across noise ($\sigma \in [0.0, 0.3]$) and delay ($\tau \in [0, 10]$) sweeps demonstrate that while deterministic heuristics severely degrade under noisy sensors (success dropping to 87.5\% with high hazard exposure), SafeExit-RL maintains a 100\% evacuation success rate and near-zero exposure, exhibiting robust hazard-anticipation and risk-averse routing.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Emergency building evacuation during structural fires requires navigating complex layouts under severe time pressure, shifting hazard fronts, and localized crowd congestion. Conventional static egress routes often direct occupants directly toward evolving fire origins or through congested choke points. Recently, reinforcement learning has emerged as a promising methodology for real-time adaptive evacuation routing~\cite{safestep}. However, many existing studies assume ideal conditions: omniscient access to exact temperature, toxic gas concentrations, and smoke distributions throughout the entire structure.

In reality, building sensor networks (e.g., IoT thermal and smoke sensors) exhibit limited coverage, sensor measurement noise, and communication latency. When path-planning algorithms receive stale or corrupted hazard estimates, naive shortest-path heuristics can fail catastrophically.

This work presents \textbf{SafeExit-RL}, an adaptive evacuation framework explicitly designed for partial observability. SafeExit-RL departs from prior literature (e.g., SafeStep~\cite{safestep}) in three foundational respects:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Sensor Degradation Model:} Occupants observe the environment only within a localized sensing radius subject to calibrated Gaussian noise and update latency.
    \item \textbf{Congestion-Aware Dynamics:} Local crowd density from concurrent evacuees dynamically impacts state representation and rewards.
    \item \textbf{Baseline Sensing Parity:} Baselines operate under the exact same noisy, delayed belief state rather than ground truth, isolating the value of RL policy learning from sensor superiority.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Problem Formulation}

\subsection{State Space and Belief Modeling}
We formulate the evacuation navigation task as a POMDP defined by the tuple $\langle \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{T}, \mathcal{R}, \Omega, \mathcal{O}, \gamma \rangle$. Let the building be a discrete 2D grid of size $H \times W$. The ground-truth state contains the occupant position $p_t = (r_t, c_t)$, the full hazard intensity grid $H_t \in [0, 1]^{H \times W}$, and all NPC evacuee positions.

The agent perceives a localized observation $o_t \in \Omega$ within sensing radius $\rho$:
\begin{equation}
o_t = \left( p_t, \{ \hat{h}(u) \}_{u \in \mathcal{N}_\rho(p_t)}, \{ c(u) \}_{u \in \mathcal{N}_\rho(p_t)}, b_t \right)
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{N}_\rho(p_t) = \{ u \mid \|u - p_t\|_1 \le \rho \}$, $c(u)$ is the count of NPC evacuees at cell $u$, and $b_t \in [-1, 1]^2$ is the unit vector pointing toward the nearest believed-safe exit.

To capture realistic instrumentation, observed hazard values are corrupted by zero-mean Gaussian noise with standard deviation $\sigma$ and updated only every $\tau$ steps:
\begin{equation}
\hat{h}_t(u) = \text{clip}\left( H_t(u) + \epsilon, 0, 1 \right), \quad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)
\end{equation}
Cells outside the sensing radius retain their stale last-known estimate in an internal memory $B_t(u)$, initialized to $0$.

For tabular reinforcement learning, the observation is discretized into a compact tuple:
\begin{equation}
s_t = \left( r_t, c_t, \text{bin}(\bar{h}_t), \text{bin}(C_t), \text{dir}(b_t) \right)
\end{equation}
where $\bar{h}_t$ is the average believed hazard across the local sensing radius binned into 4 ordinal levels (none, low, medium, high), $C_t = \sum_{u} c(u)$ is total local crowd count binned into 4 tiers ($0, 1, 2, 3+$), and $\text{dir}(b_t) \in \{0, \dots, 8\}$ quantizes exit bearing into 8 cardinal/intercardinal compass directions plus center.

\subsection{Action Space}
The discrete action space consists of 5 movement primitives:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A} = \{ \text{left}, \text{right}, \text{forward}, \text{backward}, \text{wait} \}
\end{equation}
Collisions with boundary or interior wall cells result in zero displacement and a collision penalty.

\subsection{Reward Function}
The reward function balances rapid egress, safety, and congestion avoidance:
\begin{equation}
r_t = w_1 R_{\text{prog}} + w_2 R_{\text{haz}} + w_3 R_{\text{cong}} + w_4 R_{\text{term}}
\end{equation}
Weights are parameterized as $w_1 = w_2 = w_3 = w_4 = 1.0$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $R_{\text{prog}}$ provides a per-step time cost ($c_{\text{step}} = -0.1$), a loop penalty ($c_{\text{revisit}} = -0.3$), and a wall collision penalty ($c_{\text{wall}} = -1.0$).
    \item $R_{\text{haz}} = - B_t(p_t)$ penalizes entering cells with high \emph{believed} hazard, driving subjective risk aversion.
    \item $R_{\text{cong}} = -0.2 \cdot C_t(p_t)$ penalizes delays in dense crowd clusters.
    \item $R_{\text{term}}$ executes at terminal exit arrival:
    \begin{equation}
    R_{\text{term}} = R_{\text{base}} \cdot \max\left(0, 1 - \mathcal{E}_{\text{true}}\right)
    \end{equation}
    where $R_{\text{base}} = 20.0$ and $\mathcal{E}_{\text{true}} = \sum_{k=1}^t H_k(p_k)$ is the \emph{ground-truth} cumulative exposure. Crucially, the external physical environment penalizes exposure regardless of whether the agent observed it.
\end{itemize}

\section{Environment and Algorithm}

\subsection{Hazard and Crowd Dynamics}
The layout features rooms, branching corridors, boundary walls, two distinct exits ($E$), and a fire origin ($F$).
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Synthetic Hazard Field:} Fire spreads radially from origin $x_0$ using a sigmoidal radius $R(t) = R_{\max} / (1 + \exp(-\kappa(t - t_{\text{mid}})))$ with $\kappa = 0.05, t_{\text{mid}} = 60, R_{\max} = 20$. Hazard intensity decays linearly with Euclidean distance: $H_t(x) = \max(0, 1 - \|x - x_0\|_2 / R(t))$.
    \item \textbf{Crowd Evacuation Simulation:} $N=10$ scripted NPC evacuees execute a stochastic biased random walk toward the nearest exit, creating dynamic corridor congestion.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{RL Training \& Baselines}
We train a tabular Q-learning agent with learning rate $\alpha = 0.1$, discount factor $\gamma = 0.95$, and linear $\epsilon$-greedy exploration decaying from $1.0$ to $0.05$ across 1,500 episodes (3,000 total episodes).

We benchmark against two algorithms that share the agent's exact noisy, delayed belief map:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Static Shortest-Path (Static SP):} Executes Breadth-First Search (BFS) once at $t=0$ using the initial noisy observation, treating cells with $\hat{h} \ge 0.3$ as impassable. It never replans.
    \item \textbf{Reactive Replanning (Reactive):} Recomputes BFS every $k=5$ timesteps using the current belief state $B_t$.
\end{enumerate}

At severe sensor noise ($\sigma = 0.3$), both classical baselines falter: Static SP suffers a drop in evacuation success to $87.5\%$ and dangerous exposure ($3.74$), while Reactive replanning experiences an average exposure of $4.21$ due to noisy misidentifications of safe paths. In contrast, \textbf{SafeExit-RL preserves a 100.0\% success rate with 0.00 exposure}. Because the RL agent learns generalized value gradients over state transitions, it develops structural caution against hazard-prone corridors without relying on exact instantaneous sensor readings.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
When sensors are near-perfect ($\sigma=0, \tau=0$), graph-search baselines follow optimal Euclidean geodesics, arriving in fewer steps ($9.4$ vs $17.8$). However, deterministic graph search is notoriously brittle to sensor corruption: a single false-negative reading can cause a baseline to commit to a fatal path. SafeExit-RL learns an intrinsically conservative, robust policy that circumvents unpredictable choke points.

\textbf{Limitations:} (1) The fire hazard uses an analytical proxy rather than 3D fluid-dynamic (FDS) simulation. (2) Crowd agents follow scripted stochastic rules rather than multi-agent game-theoretic equilibrium. (3) Training was conducted on a single 2D architectural footprint.

\section{Conclusion}
SafeExit-RL demonstrates that reinforcement learning provides critical resilience for indoor fire evacuation when sensory information is partial, noisy, and delayed. Under severe sensor degradation where classical heuristic graph-search degrades to 87.5\% completion with severe hazard exposure, SafeExit-RL reliably achieves 100\% evacuation safety. Future work will extend this framework to multi-floor buildings and multi-agent cooperative evacuation.
