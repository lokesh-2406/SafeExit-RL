\begin{abstract}
Most reinforcement learning (RL) evacuation models assume complete, instantaneous access to ground-truth hazard states. We propose \textbf{SafeExit-RL}, a POMDP-based adaptive evacuation framework that operates under realistic sensing degradation: partial spatial observation, Gaussian sensor noise ($\sigma$), observation staleness ($\tau$), and dynamic crowd congestion. We compare tabular Q-learning, a Deep Q-Network (DQN), and two classical graph-search baselines — all under \emph{identical} sensing limitations. Under severe sensor noise ($\sigma=0.3$), classical heuristics degrade to 87.5\% success with 3.74 cumulative hazard exposure, while RL agents maintain $>97.9\%$ success. Zero-shot cross-layout transfer further reveals that coordinate-bound tabular RL drops to 10\% success on an unseen layout, whereas continuous DQN achieves 37\%, demonstrating the necessity of layout-invariant representations.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Emergency building evacuation requires navigating complex layouts under shifting hazard fronts and crowd congestion. Static egress routes often direct occupants toward fire origins or congested choke points. While RL has emerged as a promising adaptive solution~\cite{safestep,rl_evacuation}, most prior work assumes omniscient access to exact hazard distributions~\cite{pomdp_planning}.

Real building sensor networks exhibit limited coverage, measurement noise, and communication latency. We present \textbf{SafeExit-RL}, departing from prior literature in three ways:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Sensor Degradation:} Noisy, delayed, radius-limited hazard sensing (same model for all agents).
    \item \textbf{Congestion-Aware State \& Reward:} Local NPC evacuee density impacts both state and reward.
    \item \textbf{Sensing Parity:} Baselines share the agent's exact belief map — isolating the value of \emph{learning} from the value of better sensors.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Problem Formulation}

\subsection{POMDP and Belief Modeling}
We formulate evacuation as a POMDP $\langle \mathcal{S}, \mathcal{A}, \mathcal{T}, \mathcal{R}, \Omega, \mathcal{O}, \gamma \rangle$ on a discrete $H{\times}W$ building grid. The agent perceives observations $o_t \in \Omega$ within sensing radius $\rho$:
\begin{equation}
o_t = \left( p_t,\; \{ \hat{h}(u) \}_{u \in \mathcal{N}_\rho},\; \{ c(u) \}_{u \in \mathcal{N}_\rho},\; b_t \right)
\end{equation}
where $\hat{h}_t(u) = \mathrm{clip}(H_t(u) + \epsilon,\, 0, 1)$, $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$, refreshed every $\tau$ steps. Cells outside $\rho$ retain stale belief $B_t(u)$, initialized to 0. The exit bearing $b_t \in [-1,1]^2$ points toward the nearest believed-safe exit.

\subsection{State Representations}
\textbf{Tabular Q (Coordinate):} Discretized state $s_t = (r_t, c_t, \mathrm{bin}(\bar{h}_t), \mathrm{bin}(C_t), \mathrm{dir}(b_t))$, where $\bar{h}_t$ is binned into 4 hazard levels and $C_t$ into 4 crowd tiers. \textbf{Tabular Q (Relative):} Replaces $(r_t,c_t)$ with $w_t \in \{0,1\}^4$ (local wall traversability), enabling layout-invariant transfer.

\textbf{DQN} uses a fixed 14-dimensional continuous feature vector:
\begin{equation}
\phi(o_t) = \left[ w_t,\; b_t,\; \bar{h}_t,\; \hat{h}(p_t),\; \hat{h}_\mathrm{adj},\; C_t/10,\; c(p_t)/5 \right] \in \mathbb{R}^{14}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{h}_\mathrm{adj} \in \mathbb{R}^4$ are the believed hazard values at the four cardinal neighbors. This continuous, position-invariant encoding is the key DQN advantage over tabular methods.

\subsection{Action Space and Reward}
$\mathcal{A} = \{\text{left, right, forward, backward, wait}\}$. The reward is:
\begin{equation}
r_t = R_\mathrm{prog} + R_\mathrm{haz} + R_\mathrm{cong} + R_\mathrm{term}
\end{equation}
$R_\mathrm{prog}$: step cost $-0.1$, revisit $-0.3$, wall collision $-1.0$.
$R_\mathrm{haz} = -B_t(p_t)$: penalizes believed hazard.
$R_\mathrm{cong} = -0.2 C_t(p_t)$: penalizes crowd density.
$R_\mathrm{term} = 20 \cdot \max(0,\, 1 - \mathcal{E}_\mathrm{true})$ at exit arrival, where $\mathcal{E}_\mathrm{true} = \sum_k H_k(p_k)$ is ground-truth cumulative exposure — the environment penalizes unseen risks.

\section{Environment and Algorithms}

\textbf{Layouts:} Case 1 ($9{\times}30$, 2 exits); Case 2 ($13{\times}36$, 4 exits, labyrinthine corridors). Fire spreads as $H_t(x) = \max(0, 1 - \|x-x_0\|_2 / R(t))$ with sigmoidal radius $R(t) = 20/(1+e^{-0.05(t-60)})$. $N{=}10$ NPC evacuees execute biased random walks toward exits, with configurable panic ($p_\mathrm{panic}$) and herding ($p_\mathrm{herd}$) probabilities.

\textbf{Tabular Q-Learning:} $\alpha{=}0.1$, $\gamma{=}0.95$, $\epsilon$-greedy decay $1.0{\to}0.05$ over 1,500 episodes. \textbf{DQN~\cite{dqn}:} 2-layer MLP (hidden dim 64) on $\phi(o_t)$, experience replay (buffer $10^4$, batch 64), target network sync every 100 steps, Huber loss. \textbf{Baselines:} Static Shortest-Path (BFS once at $t{=}0$) and Reactive Replanning (BFS every 5 steps), both using the identical noisy belief map $B_t$.

\subsection{In-Layout Comparison (Case 1)}
Table~\ref{tab:main} reports 100-episode evaluation ($\sigma{=}0.1, \tau{=}2$). Under standard sensing, graph-search baselines find shorter paths (9--10 steps) because they exploit global BFS from the full initial observation. However, Figure~\ref{fig:sweep} reveals the critical advantage of RL: at $\sigma{=}0.3$, Static SP drops to 87.5\% success with 3.74 exposure, while both Q and DQN maintain $>97.9\%$ success. The DQN's continuous features allow it to generalize risk-averse routing without being locked into memorized coordinate-action pairs.

\subsection{Cross-Layout Zero-Shot Generalization}
Policies trained on Case 1 are evaluated zero-shot on Case 2 (Table~\ref{tab:cross}). Coordinate-bound tabular Q collapses to 10\% success because unseen $(r,c)$ pairs have no Q-values. The relative representation doubles transfer (24\%), and the DQN's continuous $\phi(o_t)$ reaches 37\% — the highest among learned methods — because wall traversability and hazard gradients generalize structurally. Graph-search baselines retain high performance since BFS is recomputed fresh each episode.

\subsection{Noise, Delay, and Crowd Sensitivity}
Figure~\ref{fig:sweep} shows that at $\sigma{=}0.3$, Static SP drops to 87.5\% (exposure 3.74) and Reactive replanning reaches 4.21 exposure, while RL maintains 100\% with near-zero exposure. Crowd panic and herding ($p_\mathrm{panic}{=}p_\mathrm{herd}{=}0.3$) raise tabular Q exposure to 0.70, while DQN remains at 0.09, demonstrating robust crowd-avoidance learned from the continuous feature gradient.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
RL excels at learning risk-averse policies that degrade gracefully under sensor corruption. The key architectural insight is representation: coordinate-based states overfit spatial geometry; layout-invariant features (wall traversability, exit bearings, local hazard gradients) generalize structurally and are essential for practical deployment.

\textbf{Limitations:} (1) Analytical fire proxy rather than FDS physics simulation. (2) Scripted crowd rather than learned multi-agent dynamics~\cite{marl_evacuation}. (3) Single-floor 2D environment. (4) DQN needs more training data for full advantage over tabular Q on small layouts.

\section{Conclusion}
SafeExit-RL provides adaptive indoor evacuation guidance robust to partial, noisy sensing. Across two layouts and behavioral crowd perturbations, RL resists sensor degradation that defeats classical graph heuristics. Cross-layout benchmarks highlight layout-invariant representations as a prerequisite for practical transfer. Future work will incorporate graph neural networks over building topology for multi-floor scalability.
